Miles shares his knowledge of jazz with the world
=====

Miles Davis keeps an up-to-the-minute Concept of the Best Jazz Songs in the world, organized by sub genres (bebop, cool jazz, modal jazz, third stream, etc) that are being invented and reinvented in real time. Each sub genre is simply a Set within the Concept of Jazz Songs. Individual songs are items that belong to one or more of these sub genres. As these styles change and musicians evolve, he updates the sub genres and items in this Concept manually. Today he adds Third Stream, tomorrow he adds Afro-Cuban/Latin Jazz. The entire Concept lives locally and is his own “private” concept, but he makes it public, knowing outsiders will want to learn about new styles as he and his colleagues invent them. Many of his colleagues, like Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie, do the same. Indeed, there are a few styles that may be better understood by some of them, so to keep himself up to date on the latest stuff, he employs the IS_A_SUPERSET_OF relationship to connect *his* Set events to *their* Set events. Example: within their respective Brainstorms, he, Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie each have a Set node (authored individually) called *bebop*. Charlie and Dizzy curate the songs manually for their own bebop sets, but Miles, thinking Charlie the expert, simply imports all items from Charlie’s bebop subset into his own bebop subset. All this takes is a single IS_A_SUPERSET_OF connecting the two. One day, Miles decides Dizzy another expert on bebop, so pulls in Dizzy’s bebop songs using the IS_A_SUPERSET_OF relationship. Periodically, Miles’ Brainstorm combs through all items, creates an updated, curated list of Bebop Songs, and publishes them as a Trusted List that can be read by the public. Effect: Miles imports knowledge in real time from Dizzy and Charlie; the world imports knowledge in real time from Miles directly, and indirectly from Dizzy and Charlie.
